Cutting a take into hand moves, and captioning each move

Take 2026-10-28 20:14 · assembling and taking apart a small orange toy table · 251.6 s · both gloves, head camera, 3 trackers

Final pipeline

  1. Sensor check. A stream gap, an invalid tracker or a frozen sensor lasting ≥ 0.5 s marks that time unusable for the hand that needs it.
  2. Moves. Each hand's palm (midpoint of the wrist and middle-finger root) is tracked in 3D. A 2-state HMM separates "staying" from "travelling". Each movement starts at the foot of its speed rise and ends where the speed falls below 10 % of the peak. Sub-movements are joined only when they go the same way with a pause of ≤ 0.5 s. A slower one before a faster one is not joined (that rule is new). A move must end ≥ 10 cm from where it began.
  3. Length. Moves shorter than 1 s are not captioned.
  4. Hand in view. The palm is projected into the head camera for every frame of the move. The move is dropped if more than 20 % of its frames fall outside the image plus a 20 % margin. The margin absorbs the current head-camera calibration error.
  5. Caption, call A (Qwen3.5-122B, from images). The VITRA / VLA-HAND prompt gets 8 frames with the palm's future trajectory, plus three rules learned from judged errors:
    • describe only the named hand;
    • describe only what is completed in the frames;
    • among similar objects, name one only by a clearly visible feature.
    No background is given, so the action is decided from the frames alone.
  6. Caption, call B (same model, same 8 frames). B gets the background:
    • the rig (the desk, gloves and trackers are not objects);
    • the task;
    • each object with a list of names (colour/shape, everyday, technical) and its physical properties.
    B looks at the frames only to map A's nouns onto the list. It then writes up to 5 phrasings with the VLA-HAND round-2 rules: an object may take any name from its own list, and verbs change only to true synonyms. B never changes the action.

Pilot on five other tasks (A→B, both hands, 116 moves)

The tasks were apples into a bag, tissues, folding T-shirts, dealing bread slices, and bottle caps.

  • Hand not visible: in 23 % of the moves the moving hand was mostly out of the picture. The "hand in view" step above was added because of this.
  • Visible moves: the action was right in 60 % and wrong in 9 %. B improved the object names a little; everyday objects were already named well.
  • Errors found:
    • describing the other hand (an idle left hand);
    • "pick up" when the clip ends at the grasp;
    • the wrong one among identical plates.
    These led to the three extra rules in call A.

Known limits

  • One take for the cutting choices, and one judge model with 27 moves per comparison. Differences of one or two moves are not meaningful.
  • The head-camera calibration of the earlier sessions is being redone. Until then the "hand in view" test needs its margin.
  • Only the moves are captioned so far. The stretches where the hand stays in place come next.
